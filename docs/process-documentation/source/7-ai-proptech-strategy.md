## 1. The thesis in one page

**The brokerage of 2027 is an AI-native company with agents in it, not an agency that bought AI tools.** The proof is already public:

- SERHANT.'s S.MPLE platform has completed 54,000+ requests for 2,000+ agents, returned ~28,000 hours of admin work, and agents who joined and adopted it raised their commission income **144 % year on year** against their previous firm (company-reported). S.MPLE 2.0 (September 2026) adds **Dot**, an "AI chief of staff" that reads the agent's calendar, email and CRM and *starts work before being asked*, coordinating 70+ specialist agents (CMAs, listing presentations, campaigns, transaction coordination, compliance, follow-up).
- Compass agents report **15–20 hours a week** given back by an AI assistant that lives inside their pipeline and executes admin work. Zillow's **AI mode** (March 2026) lets buyers search by describing the home and takes real steps for them (tours, agent contact).
- In India, NoBroker crossed **₹1,000 crore** revenue, runs rent collection and property management at 8–10 % of rent (5–8 % on offer plans), and launched ConvoZen.AI for voice conversations; MagicBricks' READPRO AI CRM is on **85,000+ broker licences**; Housing.com prices **10 million** properties live. Landeed verifies ownership and documents with AI across states. In July 2026 WhatsApp began rolling out **Meta Business Agent** to Indian businesses — an AI that answers, qualifies and books in every Indian language — so a fast reply on chat is becoming the minimum, not the edge.
- The economics are known: a lead answered within 5 minutes converts **8–9 times better** than one answered in an hour; sub-90-second WhatsApp replies lift site-visit conversion to **25–30 %** against 5–10 %; Indian brokers who follow a structured, AI-assisted system close **4–6 %** of leads, the best **8–10 %**, against an average of 1–3 % (industry benchmarks, not audited).

3 PIN's position is unusual for a Chennai agency: the data pipeline that most brokerages lack is **already built** — chat intake, a real pipeline with milestone dates, a matching engine used in three consoles, a shoot-to-post media tracker with per-outlet cuts, a ledger per deal. What is missing is the layer that *acts* on it, the channels that *reach* buyers and sellers at scale, the media formats buyers now expect, and the recurring revenue lines that investors value more highly than commission income.

**The growth case rests on three multipliers, each measured in the CRM and Finance.**

| Multiplier | From → to (base case, A) | What moves it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Conversion** — deals per lead | 2 % → 5 % (× 2.5) | Answer in a minute on every channel, a deal score, follow-up that never lapses, briefed visits |
| **Volume** — leads and listings | 70 → 160 leads a month (× 2.3); 8 → 20 listings | Sourcing engine, distribution, the content engine, referrals |
| **Value per deal** — ticket × sides, plus services | ₹1.0 Cr × 1.2 sides → ₹1.1 Cr × 1.5 sides (× 1.4) | Exclusive listings, loans, services, management |

Multiplied, they take monthly brokerage from about ₹1.7 L to about ₹13 L, and total revenue with loans, services and management to about ₹17 L, by October 2028 — about ten times today's brokerage-only figure in the base case, five in the downside case and seventeen at benchmark-level performance (section 8). The base case adds two sales agents and the operations people the management line needs (section 22), so revenue per sales agent rises almost eight times. Every input is an assumption until Finance replaces it.

## 4. The operating model to build: "3 PIN Autopilot"

Three layers, each built on something that exists:

1. **Fact layer** — a nightly fact store (one compressed document per topic: leads, properties, deals, posts, visits) plus a Chennai price desk and demand map. Every AI reads facts from here, never raw collections (quota, speed, consistency). Exposed to Claude/ChatGPT through an MCP server, so the owner can ask any question about the business in plain Tamil or English.
2. **Agent layer** — supervised specialist agents (sourcing, seller intake, media, outreach, follow-up, transaction, finance) run by one coordinator, **Eåsy by 3 PIN**, the personal AI staff every user gets (section 12). Eåsy starts work proactively (as Dot does) and puts anything consequential into **one approval queue** the owner clears from the phone. The CRM's existing AI queue, suggestions and 7-day undo are the seed.
3. **Channel layer** — WhatsApp (TailorTalk, Flows, channels), Instagram, a phone number answered by an AI voice agent in Tamil and English, the website and **Ask Eåsy** (the buyer-facing edition of Eåsy) on WhatsApp and as a ChatGPT app, and portal feeds. Every channel writes to the same lead, the same card, the same fact store.

**Rules that never change:** a person approves Won, listings going public, agreements, prices and first contact with a new person; every AI action is logged and undoable; nothing reads customer data for a purpose it was not collected for; AI-made visuals are labelled.

### A. Sourcing — find the seller before they decide

**Current status.** Listings depend on owners contacting us. The Sellers view shows who is waiting for a card; there is no "where found" field, no shortlist stage, no outbound sourcing. Growth in listings equals growth in inbound chat volume, which costs ad money.

**What we introduce.**

| # | Initiative | Effort | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | **Likely-to-list engine** | M | A score per known owner and past contact from our own events (rent-out enquiries 11+ months old, tenants whose lease ends, buyers who bought 7–10 years ago, sellers who paused, owners whose property we marketed for rent) plus public Chennai signals (new registrations by locality from registration data, guideline-value revisions, aging rental listings on portals, new society handovers, NRI-owned flats). Transparent weights; recalibrated quarterly |
| A2 | **Owner outreach autopilot** | M | For every high-score owner, Eåsy drafts a personal WhatsApp or call script in Tamil/English with a *locality price note* ("flats like yours in Anna Nagar sold at ₹14–18k/sq ft this quarter"); the agent approves and sends; replies flow into the normal pipeline. TRAI-compliant: no cold AI calls, opt-in first |
| A3 | **Co-broking and portal feed** | S | Daily ingest from co-broking networks (CoClose has 1,500+ Chennai listings; Brokers Blok) and owner-direct portals into a "sourcing inbox"; AI de-duplicates against inventory, scores fit against open buyer demand, and proposes who to call |
| A4 | **Referral engine** | S | After every visit, deal and rental, a WhatsApp Flow asks for a referral with a one-tap share card; referrers get a tracked thank-you (and a reward where lawful). Every referral is attributed |
| A5 | **Demand-led sourcing targets** | M | The demand–supply gap engine (open buyer requirements vs stock by locality, budget and configuration) produces a weekly "find 3 x 2BHK in Velachery under 90 L" list for agents, with the exact buyers waiting |

**Why.** A brokerage's growth ceiling is listings, not buyers. Predictive sourcing is now a mature category abroad (72 % accuracy at 6–12 months); nobody in Chennai does it with owned data. Our demand data tells us *which* properties to find, which makes every outreach credible ("I have four buyers for this").

**What happens the day after.** Each agent's Daily task gains a "Sourcing" section: five owners to contact with the script ready and the buyers waiting named. The Sellers view gets a *Prospect* column before *Waiting for a card*. The owner sees a weekly "listings pipeline" number for the first time.

**How to build.** A1 is a scoring module over the fact store (section K) plus two public-data fetchers; A2 reuses the follow-up drafts and WhatsApp templates; A3 is an ingest job plus the existing duplicate detection and match engine; A4 is a WhatsApp Flow and a `referredBy` field already implied by the lead model; A5 is a report over existing match vetoes and requirements.

**Financial impact (assumption-based, section 8 model).** +25–40 % listings in year one at near-zero ad cost; each exclusive listing is worth two sides of brokerage. With 131 properties today, +40 listings a year at a 30 % sell-through and ₹1 Cr average ticket at 1 % per side ≈ **₹24 L** additional brokerage a year if both sides are ours (₹12 L if only the seller side), before referrals.

**Business impact.** Listings become a controlled input instead of luck; exclusive share rises because we arrive first with buyers in hand; the brand becomes "the agency that calls you with a buyer".

**Agent time.** +2 h/week of calls, but the research and scripting (today ~4 h/week when done at all) disappears.

**Risks.** DPDP: only contact people with a lawful basis (existing relationship, consent, public business listing); keep a do-not-contact flag everywhere. Accuracy: start with top-decile scores only; measure list rate per 100 contacts.

**Success.** Listings per month; share of listings that are exclusive; outreach-to-listing rate ≥ 5 % by month 6.

### B. Onboarding & listing — the seller autopilot

**Current status.** Listing type, documents checklist (non-blocking), brochure v1/v2 and mapping exist. Details are typed by staff; the owner's approval gate is not enforced; there is no agreement workflow, no price opinion, no instant listing presentation.

**What we introduce.**

| # | Initiative | Effort | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | **WhatsApp intake** | M | TailorTalk asks the 30-field checklist conversationally, in Tamil or English, with photos and documents as attachments; fields land on the card; staff only review |
| B2 | **Document AI and "3 PIN Verified"** | M | EC, patta/chitta, sale deed, approved plan and tax receipts are read by AI (Landeed-style, or Landeed's API); ownership chain, encumbrances and mismatches are flagged; a verified badge appears on the listing, brochure and site. Documents still never block marketing |
| B3 | **Chennai price desk** | M | A price opinion per property from guideline value (floor), our own comparables (closed deals, visit feedback, price edits), portal asks by micro-locality, and the demand map. Shown as a range with reasons; refreshed monthly; exported as a one-page "Price note" |
| B4 | **Instant listing presentation** | S | A personalised PDF and 60-second video in the owner's language: price note, buyers already matched (count and profiles), the marketing plan (shoot date, outlets, brochure), the verified badge, the agreement terms |
| B5 | **Agreement and approval gate** | S | Exclusive/Collab agreements e-signed (Leegality/Digio); the owner's one-tap approval before any agreement is drafted; a shoot for Exclusive/Collab can only be booked after sign + check (Normal skips it, as the owner's process says) |
| B6 | **Missing-paper nudges** | S | Weekly WhatsApp nudges to the seller for Missing/Applied documents, with how-to links (e.g. EC online); surfaced in Daily task |

**Why.** Sellers choose the agency that shows them a price, buyers and a plan in the first conversation. Verification is the single biggest trust gap in Indian resale and nobody local badges it. Exclusive listings double revenue per property and remove co-broking leakage.

**Day after.** A seller who messages at 9 pm has answered the checklist by 9:20, received a price note and presentation by 9:25, and seen that six buyers match. In the morning the owner sees one approval request. Staff typing drops to review.

**How to build.** B1 extends TailorTalk's prompt and the card's field model; B2 wraps a document-reading model behind the finance-upload style Drive guard; B3 is a module over the fact store, the area model and the change log; B4 is the brochure pipeline with a new template plus Sarvam TTS; B5 is e-sign API + the listing-type field; B6 reuses the follow-up digest.

**Financial impact.** Exclusive share from (assume) 20 % to 50 % of listings; each conversion adds a second side (~₹1 L per ₹1 Cr deal). Verification can be a paid service to owners (₹2,000–5,000) or a differentiator. Price desk reduces overpriced, unsellable listings (fewer wasted shoots at ₹3–5k each).

**Business impact.** Faster time-to-market (owner yes → live in 7 days instead of 2–3 weeks); fewer disputes at registration; a brand promise ("every 3 PIN listing is verified and fairly priced").

**Agent time.** −3 h/week typing and chasing per agent; the owner's approval takes 2 minutes a day.

**Risks.** Document AI must say *"needs a lawyer"* rather than guess; legal opinions stay with a panel advocate. Price notes are opinions, never valuations for lending.

**Success.** Owner-yes → live median days; exclusive share; documents complete at listing (%); seller NPS after onboarding.

**Why.** Reels are the highest-engagement format on Instagram (3.7 % average; 2.7 times carousels); 52 % of agents say social leads beat portal leads (US agent surveys; we measure our own through E3). Immersive listings are now native on the big portals; a buyer who has walked a splat tour arrives at the site visit pre-sold. Media is also the cheapest way to earn exclusives ("you cannot get this anywhere else").

**Day after.** A shoot produces ten assets instead of two; the editor's queue shows rough cuts waiting for a decision rather than raw footage; every listing has a link to send; the Tamil reel goes out the same day as the English one.

**How to build.** C1 adds kit items to `MEDIA_KEYS` and a splat processing step (hosted service or local GPU); C2/C3/C4 are API calls from the board (staging, Sarvam, LLM) with human approval; C5 is a static page generator fed from the inventory and the posting tracker; C6 sits in the Editor view.

**Financial impact.** Media cost per listing roughly flat (₹3–5k) for 5× the assets; listings with immersive media sell faster and at closer to ask (industry claims vary; we measure). Staging ₹200/image against a visit saved.

**Business impact.** Instagram reach per listing three to five times today's (target, to be measured); exclusive wins; fewer wasted visits (buyer disqualifies online).

**Agent time.** Shooter: +20 min per visit for the 360 pass. Editor: −30–40 % per cut. Owner: fewer "what should we post" decisions; the plan writes itself (Section E).

**Risks.** Staged images must be labelled (RERA/consumer protection); voice cloning needs written consent and a stored sample; splat hosting costs scale with listings (budget ₹200–500 per tour).

**Success.** Assets per shoot; leads per listing in the first 7 days; visit-to-offer rate for listings with a tour vs without.

## 6. Why this succeeds here, and what would make it fail

**Why it works.** (1) The data and the pipeline exist; most of the plan is wiring and models, not new systems. (2) Tamil-first AI is now production-grade (Sarvam, Bhashini, Google's AI Mode in Tamil), and no Chennai agency we found uses it for listings, price notes or narration. (3) The owner already fronts content and already runs the post plan — the engine formalises what works. (4) Multi-tenant, serverless and cheap to run: the stack scales without a team of engineers. (5) Each wave pays for the next (section 8).

**What would make it fail.** Building channels before facts (nothing to say); automating first contact before consent and quality (blocks, complaints); letting AI decide prices or Won; media formats without measurement; management without operations; a market slowdown met with fixed costs added too early (hires follow the wave exits, section 22); and too many parallel bets — hence the waves. The full risk register is section 25.

## 10. Guardrails and regulation

- **People decide:** Won, listings going public, prices, agreements and first contact are approved by a person; every AI action is logged and undoable.
- **DPDP Act and Rules (notified 13 Nov 2025):** consent texts for recorded calls and marketing; purpose limits; deletion schedules; breach reporting; processor agreements with every AI vendor; the main duties bind from **13 May 2027** — Wave 0 includes the texts and the flags. Registered consent managers operate from Nov 2026; using one later makes consent portable and auditable.
- **Insurance:** referrals only through a licensed corporate agent or broker partner; IRDAI's 2026 consultation proposes lower commission caps and a ban on forcing insurance with loans, so model insurance income at zero until the final rules are out.
- **TRAI:** no unsolicited AI calls; promotional calls only from a registered 140-series number and never to numbers that block real estate on the DND registry (the 1600 series is for regulated financial entities and government, not for us); outbound AI calls only to opted-in numbers.
- **TNRERA:** the agency's agent registration (₹25,000 for a firm; renewal at half) must be current and quoted on every project listing; projects on more than 500 sq m of land or with more than eight apartments must be registered before they are marketed.
- **WhatsApp:** free-form only inside the 24-hour window; approved templates otherwise; utility messages stay utility.
- **RERA and advertising:** AI-staged images labelled; facts in listings from the verified record; no invented features.
- **PMLA/KYC** once turnover passes the threshold; **SEBI**: investor products through registered platforms only; **loans** through a registered DSA.
- **AI budgets:** one daily budget per feature; a kill switch per feature; accuracy reviewed weekly.

## 14. The next chain of business — joint ventures, construction and redevelopment

**The case in one line.** A brokerage knows what sells; a developer builds what it hopes sells. With the demand map, the price desk, the buyer base and the media engine, 3 PIN knows — by locality, configuration and price band — what is short, at what price, with buyers already waiting. That is the most valuable input a development has, and it is the input 3 PIN can sell, partner on, or build on itself.

**What the market looks like.** In Chennai, joint ventures between landowners and builders typically give the landowner 40–55 % of built-up area in established areas (Valasaravakkam ~45 %+, Iyyappanthangal/Maduravoyal 40–45 %) and 35–42 % in emerging corridors; the builder funds construction, approvals and sales. Projects on more than 500 sq m of land or with more than eight apartments must register with TNRERA before marketing; smaller ones are exempt, and the agency selling registered projects needs its own TNRERA agent registration. Developer sole-selling mandates pay 2–4 % of sales against 1–2 % for ordinary channel partners. Demand data (Knight Frank, Jan–Sep 2026): ₹50 L–1 Cr is 47 % of sales; homes above ₹1 Cr rose to 42 % of sales; homes below ₹50 L fell 46 %; launches fell 7 % while sales rose 2 %, so unsold stock clears in 4.4 quarters — a market where a builder with pre-sold demand has the advantage. New formats are arriving: **built-to-suit co-living** (Truliv's 728-bed block at Navalur on OMR, built, sold to investors, leased and operated) and **senior living** (Ashiana's 22.7-acre project at Mahindra World City; Columbia Pacific across Chennai and Coimbatore) — both covered in 15.4.

**The ladder — five steps, lowest risk first.** Each step uses the previous one's data and relationships; none requires construction risk until step 4, and step 4 is deliberately small.

**Why the next twelve months, and why 3 PIN.**

- *Demand is legible now.* By Wave 2 the demand map says, per micro-locality, how many buyers wait at which budget and configuration, what they vetoed and why. A developer with that list pre-sells; one without it guesses. Pre-sold projects finance themselves.
- *Pricing is defensible.* The price desk (guideline value, comparables, asks, closings) prices units to move; the index (R1) makes 3 PIN the public reference.
- *Buyers are already in the base.* Hundreds of qualified buyers with recorded requirements convert a launch into bookings in weeks; Eåsy's catalogues do the outreach.
- *Marketing is a machine, not a cost.* Media kits, tours, Tamil narration, portals and content run per project at marginal cost.
- *Landowners are our sellers.* The likely-to-list engine finds owners whose plots suit a JV before a builder's canvasser does; the verification badge and e-sign make 3 PIN the party both sides trust.
- *Capital is lighter than it looks.* Mandates and facilitation are fee income; co-development can start with one boutique project with pre-sales covering most of construction.

**Illustrative numbers (assumptions to test on the first project).** A boutique 8-unit project in an established pocket: 8 × 1,400 sq ft × ₹12,000 = ₹13.4 Cr sales. Mandate at 3 % = **₹40 L**. Facilitation on a ₹5 Cr land JV at 1.5 % = **₹7.5 L**. A 20 % co-development stake on a 20 % project margin = **₹54 L** over the project. One such project a year, plus three mandates on others' projects at ₹1–1.5 Cr of fees each at scale (₹35–50 Cr of sales each at 3 %), would exceed the base-case brokerage line in section 8 (about ₹1.6 Cr a year) — and every unit sold enters the passport, management and resale cycle. None of this is in the section 8 cases; fees are earned as units sell, over 18–36 months, so cash arrives later than the bookings. An 8-unit project of 1,400 sq ft flats needs about 2 FSI on a 500 sq m plot; check the plot's permissible FSI before any figure is shared with a landowner.

**Branding.** "Built on demand" — projects launched with the number of waiting buyers printed on the hoarding and verified by the index; the agency that tells developers what Chennai wants. This is the story that makes developers, landowners and investors call first.

**Risks and guardrails.** No construction before two JVs have closed through step 3; a construction partner with a track record, with 3 PIN's role defined in writing (marketing, sales, liaison, data); RERA registration where thresholds apply; separate legal entity and accounts for development; capital at risk capped per project; escrow for buyer money; no price or demand claim in marketing that the fact store cannot support.
