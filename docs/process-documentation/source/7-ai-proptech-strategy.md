## 1. The thesis in one page

**The brokerage of 2027 is an AI-native company with agents in it, not an agency that bought AI tools.** The proof is already public:

- SERHANT.'s S.MPLE platform has completed 54,000+ requests for 2,000+ agents, returned ~28,000 hours of admin work, and agents who joined and adopted it raised their commission income **144 % year on year** against their previous firm. S.MPLE 2.0 (October 2026) adds **Dot**, an "AI chief of staff" that reads the agent's calendar, email and CRM and *starts work before being asked*, coordinating 70+ specialist agents (CMAs, listing presentations, campaigns, transaction coordination, compliance, follow-up).
- Compass agents report **15–20 hours a week** given back by an AI assistant that lives inside their pipeline and executes admin work. Zillow's **AI mode** (March 2026) lets buyers search by describing the home and takes real steps for them (tours, agent contact).
- In India, NoBroker crossed **₹1,000 crore** revenue, runs rent collection and property management at 8–10 % of rent, and launched ConvoZen.AI for voice conversations; MagicBricks' READPRO AI CRM is on **85,000+ broker licences**; Housing.com prices **10 million** properties live. Landeed verifies ownership and documents with AI across states.
- The economics are known: a lead answered within 5 minutes converts **8–9× better** than one answered in an hour; sub-90-second WhatsApp replies lift site-visit conversion to **25–30 %** against 5–10 %; Indian brokers who follow a structured, AI-assisted system close **4–6 %** of leads, the best **8–10 %**, against an average of 1–3 %.

3 PIN's position is unusual for a Chennai agency: the data pipeline that most brokerages lack is **already built** — chat intake, a real pipeline with milestone dates, a matching engine used in three consoles, a shoot-to-post media tracker with per-outlet cuts, a ledger per deal. What is missing is the layer that *acts* on it, the channels that *reach* buyers and sellers at scale, the media formats buyers now expect, and the recurring revenue lines that make a brokerage worth 10× more than its commissions.

**10× is not a slogan here; it is three multiplications.** Deals per lead ×3 (speed-to-lead, matching, follow-up that never lapses) · leads and listings ×2 (sourcing engine, distribution, content) · revenue per client ×1.7 (exclusive listings, loans, rentals, management, referrals). 3 × 2 × 1.7 ≈ 10, over 24–30 months, at the agent headcount of today.

## 4. The operating model to build: "3 PIN Autopilot"

Three layers, each built on something that exists:

1. **Fact layer** — a nightly fact store (one compressed document per topic: leads, properties, deals, posts, visits) plus a Chennai price desk and demand map. Every AI reads facts from here, never raw collections (quota, speed, consistency). Exposed to Claude/ChatGPT through an MCP server, so the owner can ask any question about the business in plain Tamil or English.
2. **Agent layer** — supervised specialist agents (sourcing, seller intake, media, outreach, follow-up, transaction, finance) run by one coordinator, **Eåsy by 3 PIN**, the personal AI staff every user gets (section 12). Eåsy starts work proactively (as Dot does) and puts anything consequential into **one approval queue** the owner clears from the phone. The CRM's existing AI queue, suggestions and 7-day undo are the seed.
3. **Channel layer** — WhatsApp (TailorTalk, Flows, channels), Instagram, a phone number answered by an AI voice agent in Tamil and English, the website and "Ask 3 PIN" on ChatGPT, and portal feeds. Every channel writes to the same lead, the same card, the same fact store.

**Rules that never change:** a person approves Won, listings going public, agreements, prices and first contact with a new person; every AI action is logged and undoable; nothing reads customer data for a purpose it was not collected for; AI-made visuals are labelled.

### A. Sourcing — find the seller before they decide

**Current status.** Listings depend on owners contacting us. The Sellers view shows who is waiting for a card; there is no "where found" field, no shortlist stage, no outbound sourcing. Growth in listings equals growth in inbound chat volume, which costs ad money.

**What we introduce.**

| # | Initiative | Effort | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | **Likely-to-list engine** | M | A score per known owner and past contact from our own events (rent-out enquiries 11+ months old, tenants whose lease ends, buyers who bought 7–10 years ago, sellers who paused, owners whose property we marketed for rent) plus public Chennai signals (new registrations by locality from registration data, guideline-value revisions, aging rental listings on portals, new society handovers, NRI-owned flats). Transparent weights; recalibrated quarterly |
| A2 | **Owner outreach autopilot** | M | For every high-score owner, Eåsy drafts a personal WhatsApp or call script in Tamil/English with a *locality price note* ("flats like yours in Anna Nagar sold at ₹14–18k/sq ft this quarter"); the agent approves and sends; replies flow into the normal pipeline. TRAI-compliant: no cold AI calls, opt-in first |
| A3 | **Co-broking and portal feed** | S | Daily ingest from co-broking networks (CoClose has 1,500+ Chennai listings; Brokers Blok) and owner-direct portals into a "sourcing inbox"; AI de-duplicates against inventory, scores fit against open buyer demand, and proposes who to call |
| A4 | **Referral engine** | S | After every visit, deal and rental, a WhatsApp Flow asks for a referral with a one-tap share card; referrers get a tracked thank-you (and a reward where lawful). Every referral is attributed |
| A5 | **Demand-led sourcing targets** | M | The demand–supply gap engine (open buyer requirements vs stock by locality, budget and configuration) produces a weekly "find 3 x 2BHK in Velachery under 90 L" list for agents, with the exact buyers waiting |

**Why.** A brokerage's growth ceiling is listings, not buyers. Predictive sourcing is now a mature category abroad (72 % accuracy at 6–12 months); nobody in Chennai does it with owned data. Our demand data tells us *which* properties to find, which makes every outreach credible ("I have four buyers for this").

**What happens the day after.** Each agent's Daily task gains a "Sourcing" section: five owners to contact with the script ready and the buyers waiting named. The Sellers view gets a *Prospect* column before *Waiting for a card*. The owner sees a weekly "listings pipeline" number for the first time.

**How to build.** A1 is a scoring module over the fact store (section K) plus two public-data fetchers; A2 reuses the follow-up drafts and WhatsApp templates; A3 is an ingest job plus the existing duplicate detection and match engine; A4 is a WhatsApp Flow and a `referredBy` field already implied by the lead model; A5 is a report over existing match vetoes and requirements.

**Financial impact (assumption-based, section 8 model).** +25–40 % listings in year one at near-zero ad cost; each exclusive listing is worth two sides of brokerage. With 131 properties today, +40 listings a year at a 30 % sell-through and ₹1 Cr average ticket at 1 % per side ≈ **₹24 L** additional brokerage, before referrals.

**Business impact.** Listings become a controlled input instead of luck; exclusive share rises because we arrive first with buyers in hand; the brand becomes "the agency that calls you with a buyer".

**Agent time.** +2 h/week of calls, but the research and scripting (today ~4 h/week when done at all) disappears.

**Risks.** DPDP: only contact people with a lawful basis (existing relationship, consent, public business listing); keep a do-not-contact flag everywhere. Accuracy: start with top-decile scores only; measure list rate per 100 contacts.

**Success.** Listings per month; share of listings that are exclusive; outreach-to-listing rate ≥ 5 % by month 6.

**Risks.** Document AI must say *"needs a lawyer"* rather than guess; legal opinions stay with a panel advocate. Price notes are opinions, never valuations for lending.

**Success.** Owner-yes → live median days; exclusive share; documents complete at listing (%); seller NPS after onboarding.

## 14. The next chain of business — joint ventures, construction and redevelopment

**The case in one line.** A brokerage knows what sells; a developer builds what it hopes sells. With the demand map, the price desk, the buyer base and the media engine, 3 PIN knows — by locality, configuration and price band — what is short, at what price, with buyers already waiting. That is the most valuable input a development has, and it is the input 3 PIN can sell, partner on, or build on itself.

**What the market looks like.** In Chennai, joint ventures between landowners and builders typically give the landowner 40–55 % of built-up area in established areas (Valasaravakkam ~45 %+, Iyyappanthangal/Maduravoyal 40–45 %) and 35–42 % in emerging corridors; the builder funds construction, approvals and sales. Projects over 500 sq m or more than eight units must register with TNRERA before marketing; smaller ones are exempt. Developer sole-selling mandates pay 2–4 % of sales against 1–2 % for ordinary channel partners. Demand data: 3BHK demand up 15 % in a year, mid-segment (₹50 L–1 Cr) 45 % of sales, premium 20 %.

**The ladder — five steps, lowest risk first.** Each step uses the previous one's data and relationships; none requires construction risk until step 4, and step 4 is deliberately small.

| Step | Business | What 3 PIN brings | What 3 PIN earns | Risk | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Sole-selling and launch mandates** for small and mid developers | Demand evidence for their project, a pre-qualified buyer base, the media engine, Eåsy for their sales team, verified-process branding | 2–4 % of sales value; marketing retainers | Low (effort only) | Now — use the demand map to pick 3 projects that match unmet demand |
| 2 | **Development advisory and data** | "What to build here" reports: configuration mix, pricing, absorption, buyer profile, by plot; feasibility against the price desk | ₹2–5 L per report; retained advisory; mandate on the project | Low | Q1 2027 |
| 3 | **JV facilitation and land aggregation** | Landowners from the likely-to-list engine and past clients; builders from step 1; the price desk sets a fair ratio; 3 PIN structures and documents the JV and takes the selling mandate | 1–2 % of land value as facilitation + the mandate + management of the built units | Low–medium (legal) | Q2 2027 |
| 4 | **Co-development of boutique projects** (≤ 8 units, under the RERA threshold, in demand pockets the data names) with a construction partner | Site selection from the demand map, pre-sales from the buyer base (bookings before ground-break), marketing, exit; the partner builds | Developer margin share (15–25 % of project value is the typical developer margin band; 3 PIN's share by equity and role) + mandate | Medium (capital, execution) | H2 2027 after step 3 has two JVs |
| 5 | **Redevelopment of 25–35-year-old apartments** | Society outreach (data: age, FSI headroom, demand), owner consensus tools, developer selection, 3 PIN as the marketing and resident-liaison partner | Facilitation, mandate on the surplus units, management of the rebuilt block | Medium (long cycle) | 2028 |

**Why the next twelve months, and why 3 PIN.**

- *Demand is legible now.* By Wave 2 the demand map says, per micro-locality, how many buyers wait at which budget and configuration, what they vetoed and why. A developer with that list pre-sells; one without it guesses. Pre-sold projects finance themselves.
- *Pricing is defensible.* The price desk (guideline value, comparables, asks, closings) prices units to move; the index (R1) makes 3 PIN the public reference.
- *Buyers are already in the base.* Hundreds of qualified buyers with recorded requirements convert a launch into bookings in weeks; Eåsy's catalogues do the outreach.
- *Marketing is a machine, not a cost.* Media kits, tours, Tamil narration, portals and content run per project at marginal cost.
- *Landowners are our sellers.* The likely-to-list engine finds owners whose plots suit a JV before a builder's canvasser does; the verification badge and e-sign make 3 PIN the party both sides trust.
- *Capital is lighter than it looks.* Mandates and facilitation are fee income; co-development can start with one boutique project with pre-sales covering most of construction.

**Illustrative numbers (assumptions to test on the first project).** A boutique 8-unit project in an established pocket: 8 × 1,400 sq ft × ₹12,000 = ₹13.4 Cr sales. Mandate at 3 % = **₹40 L**. Facilitation on a ₹5 Cr land JV at 1.5 % = **₹7.5 L**. A 20 % co-development stake on a 20 % project margin = **₹54 L** over the project. One such project a year, plus three mandates on others' projects at ₹1–1.5 Cr of fees each at scale, exceeds the brokerage line in section 8 — and every unit sold enters the passport, management and resale cycle.

**Branding.** "Built on demand" — projects launched with the number of waiting buyers printed on the hoarding and verified by the index; the agency that tells developers what Chennai wants. This is the story that makes developers, landowners and investors call first.

**Risks and guardrails.** No construction before two JVs have closed through step 3; a construction partner with a track record, with 3 PIN's role defined in writing (marketing, sales, liaison, data); RERA registration where thresholds apply; separate legal entity and accounts for development; capital at risk capped per project; escrow for buyer money; no price or demand claim in marketing that the fact store cannot support.

**Twelve-month sequence.** Nov–Dec 2026: demand map and price desk from existing data; pick 3 mandate targets. Jan–Mar 2027: first two sole-selling mandates; first "what to build here" report. Apr–Jun 2027: JV facilitation offer to landowners from the likely-to-list engine; first JV documented. Jul–Sep 2027: second JV; site chosen for the boutique co-development from the demand map; pre-sales list built. Oct 2027: term sheet with the construction partner; bookings opened to matched buyers. By Oct 2028: first project delivered into the passport and management lines.
